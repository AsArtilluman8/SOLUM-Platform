# SOLUM Product Strategy

## Core idea

SOLUM is not only a renderer or personal engine experiment.

SOLUM must become a product:
- a mobile-first AAA-capable engine/toolkit;
- a game foundation;
- a procedural asset economy system;
- a visual/gameplay prototyping platform;
- eventually a pitchable technology/product.

## Product question before every major system

Before building a system, ask:

1. Who needs this?
2. What problem does it solve?
3. Can it be shown in a 30-second demo?
4. Does it make SOLUM visually stronger?
5. Does it reduce asset size, development time, or technical risk?
6. Would a player, developer, investor, or partner understand the value?
7. Is it final architecture or throwaway code?

## Product lens

SOLUM must be judged as:
- technology;
- game engine;
- game prototype;
- asset pipeline;
- mobile AAA proof;
- future commercial product.

## Investor / startup lens

The useful idea from startup thinking:

Do not build only features.
Build proof of value.

For SOLUM, proof means:
- beautiful mobile renderer;
- small asset footprint;
- procedural variation;
- believable physical materials;
- strong VFX/water/world reaction;
- clear demos;
- clear roadmap;
- defensible technology direction.

## Avoid

- feature chaos;
- throwaway patches;
- model-specific hacks;
- big asset hoarding;
- unoptimized marketplace dumps;
- systems that cannot be shown visually;
- systems that do not help final SOLUM.

## Demo milestones

Good demos for future pitch/product:

1. Filament GLB renderer: car/glass/character/material preview.
2. Material Inspector: show slots, PBR extensions, overrides.
3. Procedural Asset Economy: one mesh/material creates many variants.
4. Physical Material System: wood/metal/glass/water behave differently.
5. VFX: Niagara-like magic impact with particles/trails/decals/light.
6. Water: splash/ripple/foam/interaction.
7. World reaction: grass/mud/snow/sand/water respond to footsteps/impacts.
8. Mini playable scene: character, time of day, weather, VFX, water, props.

## Rule

SOLUM should be built as something people can understand, test, want, and believe in.
